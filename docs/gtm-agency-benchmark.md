# GTM engineering agency benchmark

Research done in November 2026 to position the Quailworks site. The sources were the agencies' own homepages (The Kiln, ColdIQ, Understory) and four 2026 "best GTM engineering agencies" roundups (getaob.com, atomgtm.com, joinvalley.co, metaflow.life).

## Who sets the bar

| Agency | Positioning (their words) | Proof on the homepage | Pricing signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Kiln** (thekiln.com, acquired by 2X in Jan 2026) | "Expert GTM Engineers, at your fingertips." A team of automation experts, data scientists and early Clay employees | A 16-logo client wall right under the hero (Notion, Cognition, Gates Foundation…), case studies with hard numbers ("640+ hours of manual work per week"), and a testimonial from Clay's COO | Not on their site. Clay's partner directory lists $10k+/mo with a 3-month minimum |

GTM data API for Claude Code and agents | "300+ companies, $50M+ ARR generated", plus metric-led case cards ("$10.1M in pipeline & revenue") | Product pricing is public ($99–$4,999/mo). Agency work is a 90-day initial term |
| **Understory** (understoryagency.com) | "Allbound growth engineering". One team covering GTM engineering, paid, content and RevOps | Inc. 5000 badge, a logo row and 15+ linked case studies. It also shows an animated "how a signal becomes pipeline" diagram | Flat retainers with a 6-month minimum |
| Others in the roundups | OneGTM, Frontal, atomGTM, The GTM Engineering Company, Growth Engine X, SalesCaptain | Mostly named clients and published retainers | $3k–$10k/mo is the common band. Some publish fixed tiers ($4k/$6k) |

## The homepage pattern they all share

1. **Hero:** a one-line category claim, one sentence on who the team is, and **one CTA** ("Talk with us" or "Book a call").
2. **Social proof right away:** a logo wall or badge row directly under the hero.
3. **Services in 3 buckets:** outbound (Sales/GTM), inbound (Marketing/Growth) and RevOps/CRM. Each bucket has a concrete bullet list of builds.
4. **A visual of the system:** signal → enrich → score → route → play → CRM.
5. **Differentiators:** "small client roster", "technical people who actually ship" and "custom is our comfort zone" (The Kiln). "You own the accounts, domains and playbooks" (ColdIQ).
6. **Dated milestones:** "first play live by day 14" (ColdIQ) or "live by week 4" (Understory).
7. **Case studies with numbers, then testimonials, then a closing CTA.**
8. **Lead magnets:** a newsletter, a tool-stack page, tutorials and free tools.

## What buyers are told to look for (from the roundups)

- Real technical depth. Clay partner status is used as the vetting signal, and "rebranded SDR shops" are the warning sign.
- **Ownership.** Systems should be built in the client's own accounts, not rented.
- The signal layer: which triggers start outreach, not just the list of tools.
- Commitment terms that match the buyer's runway.

## Gaps to close next (ranked by impact)

1. **Proof.** This is the biggest gap against every benchmark. Add 2–3 case studies with numbers, even ones from earlier engineering-services work reframed as systems builds. Add client logos once you have permission. Nothing on the site is fabricated today, so this section is simply missing.
2. **A real booking link** (Cal.com or Calendly) instead of mailto.
3. **Clay Solutions Partner / HubSpot partner badges** once you've earned them. Buyers use these to vet agencies.
4. **Published starting prices.** Few peers publish them, so doing it would stand out.
5. **A lead magnet:** a newsletter or a free "GTM teardown" page. The Kiln and ColdIQ both run one.
