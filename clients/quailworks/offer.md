# Quailworks: offer and qualification (Layer 0)

Status: **offer v2, decided by Quailworks (2026-11-11)** after the first run's data and a market check,
see `decisions.md` Q29-Q33. Results change it, not questions.

## Who it is for

US technology companies (UK: industrial software only) with 11-1,000 employees that sell products worth
$20,000 or more per deal to engineering, manufacturing and operations teams: robotics and automation,
manufacturing and industrial software, industrial AI, machine vision and digital-twin software (Q29).
The moment to write: they are hiring for sales roles that find new business. That is budget already
set aside for the job this offer does.

## The offer: the 90-day Signal Engine

**In 90 days the client has its own outbound engine, running in its name, and at least 12 qualified
meetings held with the buyers it chose.** One kickoff call, then they only take the meetings.

What is in it:

1. **Market map.** Every company in their market, scored for fit, in one list they keep.
2. **Signal watch.** The reasons to write now (hiring, shows, growth, funding, certifications), checked
   against each company's own website and job board, refreshed every week.
3. **The right people.** Decision-makers found and their work emails verified.
4. **Emails from facts.** Written in the client's voice, every fact with its source, checked before it
   goes out. The client approves the first batch.
5. **Sending set-up.** Domains and mailboxes in the client's name, warmed up and monitored.
6. **Replies handled.** Every reply answered the same working day; meetings booked into their calendar.
7. **A one-page brief before every meeting.**
8. **A weekly report.** Which signal and which opening produce meetings; what we change next.
9. **Ownership.** Domains, data, playbook and a runbook are theirs. If we stopped tomorrow, it keeps running.

Bonuses (they remove the usual reasons to wait):

- **The engineer's read.** The founder, who spent over a decade as the engineer on the buying side,
  reviews their first-call deck and outreach from the buyer's seat.
- **Reactivation in week 1.** While the new mailboxes warm up, lost deals and old leads from their CRM
  are re-contacted with a current reason, so the first meetings do not wait for the warm-up.
- **The trade-show play.** Meetings booked before their next show and follow-ups after it.
- **All tools included.** No extra bills for data, sending or verification.

## The promise (Q32)

At least **12 qualified meetings held in the first 90 days of sending, or we keep working without a fee
until they have them.** It holds when: their deals are worth $20,000 or more, their market has at least
1,500 companies in the US and UK, they approve the first emails within three working days, and they
offer meeting times within five working days of a booking request. A qualified meeting is defined below
and agreed in writing before the start.

## The free first step: the Signal Sample (Q31)

Email 1 offers it, no call needed: **10 companies in their market that show a buying signal right now,
each with its source.** It shows the product instead of describing it. Built with Layers 1-2 only
(company data, no personal data), about $0.50 each. The call is offered when the list is delivered.

## Never in outreach

- Results, meeting counts achieved, response rates, prices or build times (Q3, Q33).
- Any promise other than the one above, in the exact words of email 2 (Q32).
- Client names, or the founder's past employers and programs (Q2, Q4).
- Inbound routing, CRM data or AI agents in email 1 (Q1); they come up in replies and calls.

## Pricing (calls and proposals only, never in email; Q33)

- **Founding clients (the first three):** $6,000 a month for the 90 days, no set-up fee, the promise
  above, in exchange for a named case study with real numbers. Month to month afterwards.
- **Standard:** $8,500 a month plus a $5,000 set-up fee, same promise, 90-day first term.
- **If price is the objection:** $2,500 a month plus $900 per qualified meeting held.
- **Other work** (inbound routing, CRM data): the GTM audit at $3,500, credited against a build.

## What counts as a qualified meeting

All four must be true:

1. The company matches the ICP agreed with the client in writing.
2. The person decides on, or strongly influences, buying what the client sells.
3. They replied with interest, not just curiosity.
4. The meeting took place (a no-show is rebooked and does not count).

For Quailworks' own pipeline the same test applies with `icp.yaml`; after each call the founder records
one word: qualified, not a fit, or follow up.

## Disqualify

- Fewer than 11 or more than 1,000 employees (Q10); deals under $20,000; a market under 1,500 companies
- Price-led sellers: build-to-print shops, automotive parts suppliers, engineering staffing (Q9)
- Lead-generation, marketing, sales and appointment-setting agencies (they sell what we sell)
- Staffing and recruiting firms, associations, non-profits, schools and universities, public bodies
- Companies outside the United States and the United Kingdom for now (Q8)
- The Marble Jar, and automation or software studios in the Netherlands and Belgium while it is a
  client (Q14, Q15); current clients and partners in `private/exclusions.csv`
