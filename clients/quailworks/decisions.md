# Quailworks: decisions for its own outbound (2026-11-10)

Quailworks is its own client here. Operating rule (CLAUDE.md rule 8): Quailworks decides from its
website, public data and its own results, records each decision with what would change it, and moves
on. No questionnaires. Results (replies, meetings, won deals) change decisions.

**Objective (the user, 2026-11-10): the most booked sales calls per dollar.** Every decision below is
judged on that. Tool costs are small (about $0.25 per company for data), so the levers that matter are
who we write to, what the email says, how many people we can reach per week, and how fast replies
are answered.

Sources: **quailworks.co** (the live site, read 2026-11-10; the user confirmed it is the current site,
not quailworks.xyz, which is this repository's older `index.html`); the founder's CV (shared by the
user 2026-11-10, kept out of this public repository); the Polish company register (KRS 0001108506,

## Offer and copy

- **Q1. Lead offer: signal-based outbound, built as a system.** quailworks.co sells three systems
  (signal-based outbound, inbound routing, RevOps and CRM data) and says to "start with the system that
  hurts most". Outbound leads because it is the system this engine runs, it has a live client, and the
  email itself shows the work. Inbound routing, CRM data and AI research agents come up in replies and
  calls, not in email 1. Change if: replies ask more for inbound or CRM work than for pipeline.
- **Q2. Differentiators, in this order:** (1) the founder spent over a decade as a mechanical design
  engineer on aerospace and high-performance automotive programs, choosing and managing suppliers: the
  person engineering sellers are trying to reach (CV); (2) "we were an engineering firm before we were a
  GTM firm": specs before automations, production standards (quailworks.co); (3) "you own everything",
  built inside the client's own tools (quailworks.co). Email 1 makes (1) concrete without naming
  anyone: "from launch-vehicle turbopumps to hypercar suspension" (both in the CV). Employer names stay
  out of cold email and out of this repository: naming them could read as an endorsement and may touch
  NDAs; prospects who look the founder up see them on LinkedIn anyway.
- **Q3. No claims we cannot back.** No results, meeting counts, response rates, guarantees, prices, or
  build times (the site's "typical build 2-4 weeks" stays on the site). Quailworks has one client, The
  Marble Jar, launching late November 2026, and no results yet. Change if: real results exist (then
  quote them, with the client's permission when named).
- **Q4. Proof without names.** No client names. The proof is the system itself: email 2 says this email
  came out of the same kind of system, which is true.
- **Q5. Next step: the site's own offer.** A 30-minute working session, and a written note on the three
  things we would fix first, whether or not we work together (quailworks.co contact section). Email 3
  offers a lighter alternative: 10 companies in their market showing a signal now, each with its source
  (Layers 1-2 only, company-level data, at most 5 a month, under $1 each).
- **Q6. Price: priced on value, for buyers who are not price-sensitive** (the user, 2026-11-10: target
  companies with discretionary budget and charge for the service; this replaces the earlier "first
  engagement under $3,000"). Never in email. On calls, the path on quailworks.co: a **GTM audit at
  $3,500** (1-2 weeks, fixed fee, credited in full against a build sprint started within 30 days), a
  **build sprint from $15,000** (fixed scope, quoted after the audit), and an **embedded engineer from
  $6,000 a month**. The first three clients get 20% off the build sprint in exchange for a named case
  study. Why: comparable firms charge $3,000-$10,000 a month (docs in the website branch,
  gtm-agency-benchmark.md), and for a company selling $25,000+ deals one extra won deal pays for the
  sprint. Change if: three calls in a row stall on price (audit to $2,500), or three sign without
  hesitation (raise everything by 25%).
- **Q7. Voice:** short, plain, engineer-to-engineer, in the founder's name, in English (UK spelling on
  the site; US spelling in emails to US buyers). Openings are questions that presume nothing; Jev 1.14's
  fact check falls back to a safer opening automatically.

## Targeting

- **Q8. Market: the United States for most of the volume, the United Kingdom alongside.** The US has the
  most target companies and the biggest GTM budgets (6 of 8 searches), and allows B2B cold email with an
  opt-out and a postal address (CAN-SPAM). The UK, where the founder's background carries most weight
  and one hour from the founder, gets its two strongest segments only (software and aerospace/space
  technology); PECR allows business email to limited companies with an opt-out. Ireland later; Poland
  and Germany require prior consent even for business email, so they are out. Change if: one market
  books calls at under half the other's cost after 150 contacts each (then move the volume there).
- **Q9. Who: technology companies that sell high-value products to engineering, manufacturing,
  aerospace, space and defense organizations, and can afford to pay for growth.** The test (the user,
  2026-11-10): a big market of their own, discretionary budget, and no price sensitivity. That means
  high deal values (typically $25,000+), software or high-tech hardware margins, and funding or clear
  growth: for them one booked meeting is worth thousands. Core segments: industrial and engineering
  software (LinkedIn 4, three searches: "manufacturing", "aerospace", "industrial"; US and UK),
  robotics and automation (147, 3247, 3248), test, measurement and inspection equipment (983), and
  aerospace, space and defense technology (1, 52; US and UK), where Jev 1.14's ICP fit separates
  venture-backed technology companies from build-to-print shops. The founder fit holds: their buyers
  are the engineers the founder was for over a decade (CV). **Parked as price-sensitive:** automotive
  parts suppliers (cost-down pressure from carmakers, tariffs), engineering services and UK motorsport
  engineering (staffing-like margins, small firms), precision parts and fabrication, machinery.
  Change if: the core segments run dry, or `learn` shows a parked type booking calls cheaply.
- **Q10. Size: 11-1,000 employees** (LinkedIn bands 11-50 to 501-1000), sweet spot 50-500. Why: from
  about 50 people a company has a revenue team and the budget for a retainer; venture-backed companies
  below that still qualify through the funding signal; quailworks.co's enterprise section (security,
  NDAs, procurement) supports companies up to 1,000. Change if: meetings come mainly from one band.
- **Q11. Who we email:** sales and business-development leaders first (in aerospace, the director of
  business development is often the senior seller), then marketing leaders, then the CEO, founder or
  owner at companies up to about 150 people. Never the SDRs or BDRs themselves.
- **Q12. Why now (signals):** hiring for sales roles that find new business (SDR, BDR, inside sales,
  business development, account executive, sales engineer) is the strongest signal: it is the work the
  system takes over (The Marble Jar lesson: hiring for the work you replace beats fit). Then: exhibiting
  at trade shows (US and UK aerospace, automotive, motorsport and manufacturing shows), a supplier
  certification (AS9100, IATF 16949, NADCAP, ITAR: proof they sell into aerospace or automotive
  engineering teams, and an opening of its own), **venture backing** (named investors, a Series or seed
  round on the site: budget to spend and a board asking for pipeline), a CRM or sales tool named in a
  job ad, rep or distributor-driven sales, and growth or new capacity. Weights in `scoring.yaml`.
  Change if: `learn` shows another signal converts better.
- **Q13. No lookalikes yet.** The Marble Jar (a Dutch automation studio) is not like these targets, so it
  is excluded but not used as a lookalike. Our own won deals become the list (`best-clients`).

## Sending and budget

- **Q18. Sending domains: three brand variants** of the company name: getquailworks.com,
  quailworkshq.com and tryquailworks.com (or the next available brand variant). Never quailworks.co
  itself (it carries the company's own Google Workspace mail and DMARC), never quailworks.xyz, and never
  The Marble Jar's domains. Two mailboxes per domain (six) in the founder's real name: fresh SmartSenders
  mailboxes (about $4.50 a month each), warmed up for 2-3 weeks. Pre-warmed mailboxes only come on
  unrelated domain names, which do not carry the Quailworks brand (the user, 2026-11-10). Why six and
  not four: sending capacity, not data, limits how many calls we book, and a mailbox costs less than one
  enriched company a week. The warm-up runs in parallel with discovery, scoring, contacts and drafts.
  **Own Smartlead account** (the user, 2026-11-11: keep Quailworks' GTM separate from The Marble
  Jar's, whose key is already in use elsewhere): a separate Smartlead account for Quailworks with its
  own login, billing and API key (`SMARTLEAD_API_KEY_QUAILWORKS`, `sending.yaml` `api_key_env`; Layer 6
  never falls back to The Marble Jar's key). The domains and mailboxes are bought through SmartSenders
  inside that account, because Smartlead says SmartSenders purchases cannot be transferred. Plan: Base
  ($39) or the free trial during warm-up, Pro ($94, the plan with API access and webhooks) from the
  week before the first upload. Campaigns prefixed `QW`. A client workspace inside The Marble Jar's
  account was rejected: it would share that account's API key.
- **Q19. Volume and ramp:** Tier 1-2 only, Monday to Thursday, in each market's working hours. Week 1

## Converting replies into calls

- **Q22. Speed to lead.** Interested replies are answered within one working hour in the prospect's
  market: Smartlead forwards replies to the founder's inbox, and the first answer is ready in
  `templates/replies.yaml` with the booking link. Every reply also goes through `sync` and `handoff`
  (briefs, outcomes). Why: a reply answered within the hour books far more often than one answered the
  next day, and it costs nothing.
- **Q23. Booking link.** A free Google Calendar booking page on the founder's quailworks.co Workspace
  account (Business Starter allows one), 30 minutes, in `private/sender.yaml`.
- **Q24. LinkedIn, by hand, as a second touch.** The day after email 1, the founder sends a plain
  connection request to Tier 1 contacts only (about 5 a day, 10 minutes). No automation tool: the
  founder's single profile is the asset, and engineers check it. The headline must say Quailworks
  (former design engineer, now GTM engineering), or the email and the profile disagree.
- **Q25. Warm list first.** The founder writes personally to 20-30 people from past programs who now
  sell into engineering teams (suppliers' sales and business-development people). Free, highest
  conversion, outside the engine (no personal data from it enters the database).
- **Q26. Website match.** quailworks.co says "GTM engineering for revenue teams" and shows no
  engineering sellers, so a prospect who clicks through sees a generic agency. Proposed: one section
  for companies that sell to engineering teams, plus the founder's background. Prepared for the user's
  OK before it goes live (public change).
- **Q27. Scale and stop rules, in booked calls per dollar.** Weekly `learn`. After 100 contacts in a
  segment or market: under 1 positive reply, change the opening or drop the segment; 3% or more
  positive replies, give it the volume. Tier 3 is enriched only when Tier 1-2 is used up and the
  message works. Apify can go back to the free plan in months without discovery.

- **Q28. Data source test: Apollo next month, against Harvest.** Harvest (Apify) stays the company
  source for the current Apify Starter cycle (paid, ends 9 December 2026). In the next cycle, one Apollo
  seat for one month (about $49-59) runs the same segments, measured on three things: share of companies
  reaching Tier 1-2, good-fit companies Harvest missed, and bounce rate on 50 Apollo emails against
  FullEnrich. Why: Harvest's LinkedIn keyword search matches company names (the first live test needed
  hand-tuned phrases), while Apollo filters on description keywords, funding, technologies and job
  postings, which are our signals. FullEnrich stays the email source either way. Decide before the Apify
  renewal: if Apollo wins clearly, it becomes a second Layer 1 source and Apify can drop back to the free
  plan; if not, cancel the seat. BlitzAPI (flat from $99 a month) only pays off at about ten times our
  volume; GetLeads.io has no API for the engine.
